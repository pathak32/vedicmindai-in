# Reasoning & Aptitude — Full Curriculum Design
*40 chapters across 4 levels, mirroring the existing Vedic Maths structure*

## Why R&A deserves equal weight to Vedic Maths

Vedic Maths sutras are finite — there are only so many calculation tricks, and once
a student learns Nikhilam or Ekadhikena, repetition is what builds speed, not new
concepts. Reasoning & Aptitude is structurally different: it has genuine depth to
climb — simple classification puzzles at Class 3 level all the way to permutation,
probability, and multi-statement syllogisms at competitive-exam level. This makes
R&A the stronger long-term retention engine: students who master all the sutras can
*still* have years of harder reasoning content ahead of them, which is exactly what
keeps a student staying "until their education is sorted," as you put it.

---

## Structure: 4 Levels × 10 Chapters = 40 Chapters

Each level roughly maps to a class range, but a student advances by mastery, not
age — a sharp Class 5 student can reach Level 2 early, which is good; it rewards
genuine ability rather than gatekeeping by grade.

---

## LEVEL 1 — Foundations (roughly Class 3–5)

**1. Odd One Out (Classification)**
*Spot which item doesn't belong with the rest.*
Example: Apple, Banana, Carrot, Mango → **Carrot** (not a fruit)
Why it matters: builds the core reasoning skill of grouping-by-attribute, the base
of almost all later logical categorization.

**2. Simple Number Series**
*Find the pattern and the next number.*
Example: 2, 4, 6, 8, ? → **10**
Progression hook: this chapter alone can span years — simple +2 patterns here,
squares/alternating patterns by Level 2, and geometric/combined patterns by Level 4.

**3. Basic Analogies**
*If A is to B, then C is to ?*
Example: Hand : Glove :: Foot : ? → **Shoe**
Builds relational thinking — a core skill tested in nearly every competitive exam.

**4. Ranking & Ordering**
*Simple position-based puzzles.*
Example: Rahul is taller than Priya. Priya is taller than Aman. Who is shortest? → **Aman**

**5. Direction Sense (Basic)**
*Simple path-tracing with N/S/E/W.*
Example: Walk 5m North, then 3m East. How far are you from start? (Just directional
understanding at this level, not Pythagoras yet.)

**6. Basic Coding-Decoding**
*Simple letter-shift codes.*
Example: If CAT = DBU, then DOG = ? → **EPH**

**7. Calendar Basics**
*Days of the week, simple date reasoning.*
Example: If today is Monday, what day is it after 10 days? → **Thursday**

**8. Pattern Completion (Visual)**
*Complete the shape/figure sequence.*
Non-verbal reasoning — important because it's language-independent and often a
student's first taste of "reasoning feels different from maths, and I might be
good at it even if I struggle with calculation."

**9. Basic Blood Relations**
*Immediate family only.*
Example: "This is my father's son, but not me." Who is it? → **Brother**

**10. Mirror Images (Basic)**
*What does this look like in a mirror?*
Simple, visual, satisfying — a good "fun chapter" to close out Level 1 before the
jump in complexity at Level 2.

---

## LEVEL 2 — Building Blocks (roughly Class 5–7)

**1. Number Series (Advanced)**
Squares, cubes, alternating operations.
Example: 1, 4, 9, 16, 25, ? → **36**

**2. Coding-Decoding (Word & Number)**
Example: If MONEY = NPOFZ, then HONEST = ? → **IPOFTU**

**3. Blood Relations (Extended Family)**
Generation puzzles — grandparents, in-laws, cousins.

**4. Clock Problems**
Angle between hour and minute hands, simple time-gap puzzles.

**5. Ratio & Proportion**
Example: Divide ₹600 in the ratio 2:3:5.

**6. Percentage Basics**
Example: What is 20% of 350?

**7. Average**
Example: Find the average of 12, 18, 24, 30.

**8. Profit & Loss (Basic)**
Example: Bought for ₹100, sold for ₹120. Profit %?

**9. Simple Interest**
Example: ₹800 at 10% for 2 years — find SI.

**10. Venn Diagrams (Basic Set Logic)**
"How many students play both cricket and football?" style questions.

---

## LEVEL 3 — Real Depth (roughly Class 7–9)

**1. Time, Speed & Distance**
Example: A train covers 60km in 45 minutes. Speed in km/h?

**2. Time & Work**
Example: 5 workers finish a wall in 12 days. How long for 10 workers?

**3. Compound Interest**
Building on Level 2's Simple Interest.

**4. Mixture & Alligation**
Example: Mixing two solutions of different concentrations.

**5. HCF & LCM Applications**
Word-problem style, not just calculation.

**6. Syllogisms (Basic)**
"All A are B. All B are C. Therefore, all A are C." — introducing formal logic.

**7. Seating Arrangement (Linear)**
5-6 people in a row, clue-based positioning — a genuinely fun puzzle format.

**8. Data Interpretation (Bar/Pie Basics)**
Reading and calculating from simple charts — a skill directly useful for school
exams and beyond.

**9. Number System (Divisibility, Remainders)**
Deeper than basic arithmetic — properties of numbers.

**10. Statement & Conclusion**
"Does the conclusion logically follow the statement?" — critical thinking chapter.

---

## LEVEL 4 — Competitive Mastery (roughly Class 9–10 / competitive exam prep)

**1. Seating Arrangement (Circular & Complex)**
Multi-clue circular arrangements — genuinely challenging and satisfying to solve.

**2. Puzzles (Multi-Variable)**
Grid-based logic puzzles combining multiple clue types.

**3. Permutation & Combination (Basics)**
First formal introduction — "how many ways can 5 people be arranged?"

**4. Probability (Basics)**
Coin, dice, and card problems.

**5. Advanced Data Interpretation**
Multi-chart, multi-step calculation problems.

**6. Syllogisms (Advanced)**
Multiple statements, "some/none" logic, competitive-exam level.

**7. Cause & Effect / Critical Reasoning**
"Which of these is the most likely cause of the given effect?" — genuinely advanced
critical thinking, rare in most school curricula.

**8. Mensuration (Applied)**
Areas, volumes — reasoning applied to geometry.

**9. Algebra-Based Reasoning**
Puzzles solved via simple equations, bridging maths and logic.

**10. Mixed Competitive Aptitude (Capstone)**
A mock-test-style chapter mixing all Level 4 topics — the "final boss," positioned
as prep-relevant for SSC/banking/school-level competitive exams.

## UI/UX Recommendations

**Chapter card structure (matching your existing lesson format):**
Each chapter should follow the same 3-step flow already working well for Vedic
Maths lessons:
1. **Concept card** — one-paragraph explanation + one worked example, visually
   simple (a colored icon per chapter type: series/logic/quant/etc.)
2. **Try it yourself** — 2-3 guided practice questions with immediate feedback
3. **Chapter quiz** — 5-10 questions, unlocks next chapter on completion

**Difficulty badges:** Use the same visual language as your sutra levels (e.g., a
small colored dot or tag: green=foundational, amber=building, red=advanced) so a
student instantly recognizes where they are.

**Category icons, distinct from Vedic Maths:** Since sutras use a "🕉️/abacus" visual
language, R&A should get its own consistent icon set — e.g., a magnifying glass for
classification, a puzzle piece for arrangements, a clock for time-based chapters —
so the two subjects feel like siblings, not clones.

**Progress-and-retention hooks:**
- A visible **"chapters remaining"** counter per level — since R&A has real depth,
  showing "31 more chapters to Competitive Mastery" is a genuinely motivating,
  honest long-term goal (unlike a finite sutra list that runs out).
- Tie into Battle Mode and Quiz Squad — R&A chapters are naturally suited to
  1v1 battles once a student unlocks them, reinforcing the "stay because there's
  always another challenge" design goal you described.
